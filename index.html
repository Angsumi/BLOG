EGO

Ego is a double edged sword that can hurt the individual or their surroundings. Or it may leverage you by compelling you to do hard work for status.

I see everything in a technical way. Ego is neither bad nor good. If our body is our physical identity and shape, ego is our psychological identity and shape.

We have named ego in multiple ways throughout human history and across cultures:

Swabhiman (স্বাভিমান): Dignified self-respect rooted in inner truth. Not needing to diminish anyone else to stand upright.

Knowledge × Ego

③ Narcissism The Peak

A little knowledge creates a steep, fragile peak. The mind confuses initial understanding with complete mastery. Some people stay stuck here.

Why study ego? Because of three things:

1

The ancient core: Ego was the central inquiry of ancient philosophy and religion. Hinayana (original Buddhism) taught the dissolution of ego to reach Nibbana. Sufism sees ego as the singular obstacle between self and the divine. Stoicism saw it as nature’s way to compel us to live, fight, and create.

2

The hidden operating system: Ego is the invisible kernel of our psyche. What, who, and how we love or hate is determined by our ego. It sits within as a restless engine—never letting us stop, never setting us free, constantly calculating status and competition in society.

3

Obstacle and path at the same time: Manage your ego like the ambitious do—it gives immense energy to work and achieve. But remember: as Krishna said in the Gita, the lagam (the reins) must stay with you, not with the ego.

Consciousness directs the drive. Energy fuels craft, clarity, and perseverance without fragile status anxiety.
“Ego is the obstacle & Ego is the path at the same time.”
